Baseline Findings: Tidal Retention and Release

A demonstration research brief summarizing two years of microplastic observations across an urban estuary.

Coastal Signal Lab18 March 2026Version 1.0

The baseline survey indicates that marsh edges are not passive boundaries. They temporarily retain small particles, reshape exposure, and delay movement toward the coast.

Study frame

Eighteen stations were sampled across nine tide cycles, spanning upper-estuary stormwater inputs, vegetated marsh edges, the central channel, and the coastal outlet. Each sample was filtered, imaged, and classified by polymer when material quality allowed.

Primary findings

Retention was highly local

Vegetated inlets showed the strongest short-term retention. Fibers under 2 mm were more likely to remain than larger, rigid fragments under comparable current speeds.

Rain changed the timing

Particle flux rose sharply during the first ebb tide after rainfall. The modeled peak occurred four to seven hours after maximum stormwater discharge, suggesting that sampling only at peak rain would miss the principal movement window.

Fibers dominated the classified set

Polyester and nylon represented nearly half of the classified particles. Their distribution was strongest in the upper estuary and declined toward the coastal outlet.
