From Sample Bottle to Spectral Match

A transparent demonstration workflow for collection, contamination control, imaging, and polymer identification.

Coastal Signal Lab04 November 2025Protocol 2.3

The protocol was designed to make every classification traceable from a station and tide state to a stored image, spectrum, and confidence score.

1. Field collection

Surface water was collected in three replicate glass bottles at each station. Bottles were rinsed with filtered water before deployment and opened only at the sampling depth. Station time, tide stage, weather, and current direction were recorded in the field log.

2. Contamination control

  • Cotton lab coats and nitrile gloves were used during processing.
  • One procedural blank was included for every six environmental samples.
  • All solutions were filtered through 0.7 μm glass-fiber filters.
  • Open dishes were covered whenever active handling stopped.

3. Imaging and measurement

Suspected particles were photographed at consistent magnification against a calibrated scale. Analysts recorded morphology, color, maximum dimension, and minimum dimension before spectral analysis.

4. FTIR classification

Attenuated total reflectance spectra were collected from 4,000 to 650 cm⁻¹. Automated library matches were reviewed by an analyst, with special attention to characteristic peaks and evidence of weathering.

5. Quality flags

Each record carries a contamination flag, imaging-quality flag, and spectral-confidence field. Blank-corrected totals exclude particles matching the dominant color and morphology observed in the corresponding procedural blank.

Reproducibility

The demonstration dataset uses the same column structure described here. A real project can extend the repository with raw images, spectral files, analysis notebooks, and a versioned data dictionary.
