Project Summary

Limited transportation options can make grocery shopping difficult for low-income households, seniors, and people with disabilities. This study surveys residents in Santa Ana, CA, and Syracuse, NY, to understand travel patterns and preferences for grocery access, including access to co-ops, mobile markets, and delivery services. Results will guide strategies to improve access to healthy food in underserved communities.

The findings and community briefs presented on this site are drawn from 476 valid survey responses collected in Syracuse, New York, from October through December 2025. They explore health and wellness, grocery shopping overall, and grocery trips by city bus.

What residents told us about fresh food access.

These findings reflect the Syracuse, New York survey. Together, the three briefs show that food access is not a single issue: household budgets, health, neighborhood retail, and transportation all influence whether fresh food is within reach.

Syracuse survey · October–December 2025 · 476 valid responses · 421 in person · 55 online

Community brief I · 01

Grocery Shopping Overview

This brief describes where households shop, what makes grocery shopping difficult, and the food retail and transportation options residents say would improve access to fresh food.

92.2%use full-service grocery stores
62.4%identify expensive groceries or produce as a barrier
59.5%would like a neighborhood farmers market

How respondents grocery shop

  • Full-service grocery store92.2%
  • Farmers market40.3%
  • Dollar store38.4%
  • Food pantry33.0%
  • Online with home delivery18.7%

Barriers and resident priorities

After affordability, respondents most often identified limited fresh or healthy options (31.3%), high transportation costs (22.9%), and lack of time (20.8%).

How to read these percentagesThe share of respondents who said each option would improve their access to fresh food.

  • Full-service grocery store65.4%
  • Neighborhood farmers market59.5%
  • Neighborhood/backyard garden37.0%
  • Food co-op34.2%
  • Improved public transportation30.0%

Community brief II · 02

Nutritional Health and Wellness

This brief summarizes residents’ answers about food security, self-described health, stress, diet-related conditions, and beliefs about the role of fresh food in supporting health.

58%experience low or very low food security
40%describe their health as fair or poor
78%say fresh, nutritious foods reduce stress

Food security in the last 12 months

  • Food bought did not last and there was no money for more59%
  • Could not afford to eat balanced meals56%
  • Ate less than they felt they should38%
  • Were hungry but did not eat because food money ran out29%

Health and the perceived benefit of fresh food

35% reported inadequate access to mental health care. The most common diet-related issues were hypertension (23.1%) and type 2 diabetes (14.9%).

How to read these percentagesThe share of respondents who believe fresh food can help address each health condition.

  • Stress77.7%
  • Type 2 diabetes66.6%
  • Heart disease64.7%
  • Hypertension61.3%
  • Stroke risk57.1%

Community brief III · 03

Grocery Shopping by City Bus

This brief focuses on how residents use city buses for grocery trips, the barriers riders face, and practical changes that could make fresh food easier and safer to reach.

21.8%do not have weekly access to a car
15.2%sometimes or always take the bus to grocery shop
30.0%want public transportation improved for grocery shopping

Barriers among those seeking improvements

How to read these percentagesThese percentages are calculated only among the 30% of respondents who said improved public transportation would help them access fresh food.

  • Difficulty carrying groceries onto buses52.4%
  • Bus stops too far from stores25.9%
  • Bus stops too far from home25.9%
  • Inadequate bus stop shelters25.2%
  • Insufficient or poor sidewalks22.4%
  • Unsafe bus stops21.7%

Changes residents said would help

Among respondents who felt unsafe using public transportation, 61% wanted better lighting, 56% designated grocery storage, 55% improved security, and 53% a way to secure a shopping cart next to them.

How to read these percentagesThese percentages are calculated only among the 30% of respondents who said improved public transportation would help them access fresh food.

  • Free bus fares for grocery trips73%
  • Grocery discounts or a folding cart53%
  • Help carrying groceries on and off44%
  • Improved disability access38%
  • Bus trip-planning app35%
